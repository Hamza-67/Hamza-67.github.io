Assembly Routing Time-Reduction Allocation

A decision-support model for allocating a finite improvement target across heterogeneous assembly operations while respecting feasibility, quality risk, and operation-level reduction caps.

01 / Problem

Improvement capacity is limited — allocation should be explicit.

Industrial routings contain operations with different durations, constraints, improvement potential, and quality exposure. A uniform percentage reduction ignores these differences and can produce recommendations that are infeasible or operationally unsafe.

The model therefore treats the total reduction target as a scarce resource to allocate, rather than assuming that every operation can absorb the same effort.

02 / Method

A transparent constrained allocation model.

Each operation receives a reduction variable gᵢ. The objective penalizes large relative reductions on sensitive or highly weighted operations while satisfying the total target and individual feasibility caps.

min Σ Wᵢ (gᵢ / Tᵢ)²
subject to Σgᵢ = G and 0 ≤ gᵢ ≤ capᵢTᵢ: operation duration · Wᵢ: priority/risk weight · G: total improvement target · capᵢ: feasible reduction ceiling
  1. Structure the routing and define operation-specific features.
  2. Construct a transparent weighted baseline.
  3. Solve the constrained nonlinear problem with SLSQP.
  4. Flag operations at or near their feasible caps.
03 / Validation

Stress-tested under uncertainty, not presented as certainty.

A 5,000-run Monte Carlo procedure perturbs the relevant assumptions and checks whether the recommended allocation remains feasible and structurally stable. The goal is not to claim a single exact answer, but to identify recommendations that remain defensible across plausible conditions.

Confidentiality boundary

The public case study reproduces the reasoning and mathematical structure using synthetic, representative inputs. It does not expose Safran production data, routing details, operation names, or proprietary thresholds.

04 / Limitations

The model supports engineering judgment; it does not replace it.

  • Weights and caps require expert review and should not be treated as objective facts.
  • Interactions between operations may require a richer model than separable penalties.
  • Implementation feasibility must be confirmed on the shop floor.
  • The public example cannot reproduce the confidential industrial dataset.
