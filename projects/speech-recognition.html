French Number Recognition from 0 to 99

A speech-recognition system that learns reusable acoustic components with a CNN, then composes them into valid French numbers through silence segmentation and constrained linguistic decoding.

01 / Problem

Recognizing 100 labels directly wastes the structure of the language.

French numbers are compositional: many outputs reuse the same acoustic elements, while their order is governed by grammar. Treating every number from 0 to 99 as an independent class requires more data and ignores this reusable structure.

The project instead learns atomic spoken components and delegates valid composition to an explicit decoder.

02 / Architecture

A hybrid acoustic and linguistic pipeline.

16 kHz audio→Silence segmentation→64-bin mel→CNN components→Constrained decoder

Acoustic model

Audio is converted into normalized mel spectrograms. A convolutional neural network predicts atomic number components rather than complete 0–99 labels.

Constrained decoding

The decoder maps predicted component sequences to valid French numbers and rejects combinations that violate the supported grammar. For example, a sequence corresponding to quatre · vingt · dix · sept is composed into 97.

03 / Validation

Evaluation separates classification from composition.

The experimental protocol uses a held-out split and checks performance at multiple levels: component classification, complete-number decoding, confusion patterns, and behavior on speakers not represented in the training subset.

  • Early stopping controls overfitting during CNN training.
  • Confusion matrices identify acoustically similar components.
  • Decoder errors are separated from acoustic-model errors.
  • Speaker generalization is treated as a distinct test, not assumed from aggregate accuracy.
04 / Limitations

Small-data speech recognition remains sensitive to recording conditions.

  • The dataset is limited in speakers, accents, microphones, and acoustic environments.
  • Silence-based segmentation can fail with rapid or connected speech.
  • A constrained grammar improves validity but cannot repair every acoustic error.
  • The architecture is designed for the bounded 0–99 task, not unrestricted speech recognition.
