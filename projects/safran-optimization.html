Nacelle Assembly Allocation under Constraints

A self-initiated study of how multicriteria scoring, convex quadratic programming, and Monte Carlo analysis can allocate a theoretical time-reduction target across modeled assembly operations.

01 / Context

Industrial observation became a separate modeling question.

My Safran Nacelles internship focused on tooling flows, assembly-method interfaces, specifications, and CAD work. After the internship, I developed this independent study to examine a broader operations-research question using representative, anonymized modeled data. It is not presented as a deployed Safran tool or a realized production result.

02 / Problem

A global reduction target must become feasible operation-level decisions.

Uniformly reducing every task ignores feasibility, variability, technical difficulty, and quality constraints. The study therefore treats the target as an allocation problem: decide where modeled improvement effort should be concentrated while bounding the change assigned to every operation.

03 / Method

Combine engineering criteria with constrained optimization.

36 operations→Criteria→Scores→Convex QP→Allocation
  1. Describe each modeled operation through representative time, variability, technical difficulty, and improvement-potential indicators.
  2. Aggregate the criteria into a documented priority score.
  3. Formulate a convex quadratic objective that avoids concentrating the entire target on a small number of tasks.
  4. Impose a total-reduction equality and operation-level lower and upper bounds.
  5. Check the numerical solution against feasibility and constraint residuals.
04 / Robustness

Stress-test the allocation rather than trusting one optimum.

A 5,000-scenario Monte Carlo experiment perturbed the modeled operation characteristics and recomputed outcome indicators. The purpose was to identify whether the allocation remained stable under uncertainty and which assumptions drove the largest changes.

Interpretation

The scenarios test mathematical robustness within the modeled assumptions. They do not establish production savings without implementation, observation, and shop-floor validation.
